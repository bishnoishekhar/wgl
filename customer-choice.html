Customer Choice

You can buy natural gas from a licensed energy supplier or stay with Washington Gas. Either way, Washington Gas delivers it.

Who can take part

All customers in Washington, D.C., Maryland and Virginia can choose a licensed energy supplier, and you do not have to buy gas from Washington Gas. If you choose a supplier, you sign an agreement with that supplier for the terms of your service (the FAQ calls it a consent form authorizing the supplier to enroll you).

Suppliers do not work for Washington Gas. They compete for your business, so prices and services will differ.

Who licenses suppliers

  • D.C.: the Public Service Commission of the District of Columbia, 202-626-5100.
  • Maryland: the Maryland Public Service Commission, 410-767-8000 or 800-492-0474.
  • Virginia: the Virginia State Corporation Commission, 804-371-9967 or 800-552-7945.

What Washington Gas still does

Whichever supplier you pick, Washington Gas continues to:

  • deliver gas safely and reliably through its distribution system;
  • read your meter and address your customer needs;
  • bill and collect for usage;
  • respond around the clock to natural gas emergencies.

Washington Gas also supplies the gas to any customer who does not choose a supplier. Gas emergencies: leave the area, then call 911 and 844-WASHGAS (844-927-4427), option 1.

Billing

Billing options

Depending on the billing services your supplier offers, you may get one bill or two. Discuss billing choices with prospective suppliers.

One bill

Combined charges

Washington Gas charges and the supplier’s charges together. The bill may come from Washington Gas or from the supplier. If you choose a combined bill from Washington Gas, Maryland regulations require Washington Gas to purchase the supplier charges, and they become utility charges due to Washington Gas.

Two bills

Separate bills

Washington Gas bills the distribution charge, system charge and applicable taxes. The supplier bills the gas supply and any other applicable charges.

Maryland payments on a combined bill

Maryland regulations currently apply payments in this order: past-due Washington Gas utility charges, past-due supplier gas charges, current Washington Gas charges, current supplier charges, then any other non-regulated purchase on the bill.

The Maryland market is changing

The Customer Choice page says that, effective January 1, 2025, Maryland law requires gas suppliers to perform their own billing and to price their product lower than the monthly price charged by gas utilities, and that it is aware all suppliers have or will soon exit the Maryland market. The FAQ page still describes enrollment as open every month, so ask Washington Gas which suppliers are currently available.

Before you sign

Questions to ask energy suppliers

Shop around and compare prices and services, as with any other important purchase. These are the questions the Washington Gas FAQ suggests.

Licensing

Are they approved?

  • D.C.: licensed by the Public Service Commission of the District of Columbia?
  • Maryland: approved by the Maryland Public Service Commission?
  • Virginia: approved by the Virginia State Corporation Commission?
Pricing

How is the price set?

  • Fixed price or does it change? If fixed, for how long?
  • If variable, how often does it change?
  • Does the price depend on how much gas I use?
  • Does it include balancing and interstate transportation charges?
Fees

Any extra charges?

  • Is there an enrollment fee?
  • Is there a fee if I cancel early?
  • Are there any other fees, and which charges are in the quoted price?
Agreements

What do I sign?

  • What do I have to sign?
  • How long is the contract?
  • What happens when it expires or if I cancel?
Billing

One bill or two?

  • Will I get one bill or two separate bills?
  • Is there a budget plan or other billing option?
Comparing

Like for like

  • Ask whether the quoted price includes the balancing charge. The Washington Gas price already includes it.
  • Washington Gas does not offer a fixed price for one or two years, so its per-therm price can change monthly.

Opt out of supplier contact

Maryland and Virginia: you can ask to be left off the customer list that Washington Gas gives to licensed suppliers (typically sent twice a year, in spring and fall). You can ask online or by calling 844-WASHGAS (844-927-4427).

Maryland residential customers: the Do Not Transfer List blocks your account from being automatically switched to a third-party supplier.

Opt-out links on the official Customer Choice page (opens in a new tab)

Switching back to Washington Gas

In Maryland, switching to Washington Gas has no charge and you contact your current supplier to make the change. If you ask in the first 20 days of your billing cycle, Washington Gas becomes your default supplier the next calendar month. If you ask in the last 10 days, you may need to stay with your current supplier for an additional month.

Official pages: Customer Choice (opens in a new tab), Customer Choice FAQs (opens in a new tab). Read on October 17, 2026. Supplier lists and prices change, so use the official page for current suppliers.
